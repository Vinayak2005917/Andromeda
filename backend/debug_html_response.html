01

Sights

tap a card, no tap needed
Historic

Senso-ji Temple

Tokyo's oldest temple in Asakusa. Giant red lantern, incense smoke, and a shopping street that has sold snacks since 1688. Go early before the crowds inherit the earth.

Iconic

Shibuya Crossing

The scramble crossing that made every movie think Tokyo is a video game. Best viewed from the Starbucks balcony or the Shibuya Sky deck at sunset.

Peaceful

Meiji Shrine

A forest in the middle of the city, dedicated to Emperor Meiji. Walk the gravel path, do the shrine rituals, and appreciate that you're 10 minutes from Harajuku's chaos.

Views

Tokyo Skytree

634 meters of "we built this just to prove we could." Two observation decks, one very long elevator ride, and the best free view of Mt. Fuji on a clear day.

Digital Art

teamLab Planets

Walk through water, hang out with digital flowers, question reality. Book tickets in advance or accept your fate of standing in line looking at your phone.

Otaku HQ

Akihabara

Eight floors of electronics, anime figurines, and arcades. The neon density is off the charts. Bring cash, leave with a plastic katana you absolutely needed.

02

Food

your wallet will cry, your taste buds won't
Must Try

Ramen

Tonkotsu, shoyu, miso, tsukemen — pick your poison. Ichiran for solo booths, Ippudo for fame, or a random basement shop for the real deal. Slurping is mandatory, not rude.

Classic

Sushi

Tsukiji's outer market for morning bites, or a kaiten (conveyor belt) spot for budget glory. Omakase if you're feeling fancy and/or financially reckless.

Street Food

Asakusa Snacks

Melon pan, taiyaki (fish-shaped red bean cakes), ningyo-yaki, and senbei. Basically a carb festival disguised as a temple district.

Late Night

Izakaya

Japanese pub food: yakitori skewers, karaage, and cold beer under paper lanterns. Omoide Yokocho in Shinjuku is the atmospheric gold standard.

Comfort

Japanese Curry

CoCo Ichibanya is a chain, sure, but it's a glorious one. Choose your spice level, add cheese, top with pork cutlet. This is peak comfort food, no notes.

Sweet

Matcha Everything

Soft serve, lattes, mochi, cake, crepes. If it can be matcha'd, Tokyo has matcha'd it. Harajuku crepes are a tourist rite of passage.

3-Day Itinerary

pick a day
Day 1 — Classic East Tokyo

Old Meets Weird

08:30

Senso-ji & Nakamise Street

Beat the crowds, get the temple photos without 400 strangers photobombing you.

10:30

Sumida River Walk

Stroll toward the Skytree or grab a river cruise for a different angle of the skyline.

12:00

Lunch: Monjayaki or Ramen

Tsukishima for monjayaki (savory pancake you cook yourself, messy and delicious) or a quick ramen pit stop.

14:00

Ueno Park & Museums

Pandas at the zoo, art at the Tokyo National Museum, and an excuse to walk off lunch.

16:30

Akihabara

Arcades, anime, and the world's most aggressive electronic billboards. Try a crane game, lose your dignity.

19:30

Dinner in Asakusa / Skytree at Night

Back to the old town for dinner, then Skytree after dark. The city lights are a whole mood.

Day 2 — Modern West Tokyo

Chaos, Fashion, Neon

09:00

Meiji Shrine

Quiet forest energy to prepare your soul for the rest of the day's insanity.

11:00

Harajuku & Takeshita Street

Crepes, quirky fashion, and so many teenagers. Feel old, buy a crepe anyway.

13:00

Lunch: Omotesando Area

Fancy architecture, decent cafes, and the chance to pretend you're an influencer.

15:00

Shibuya Crossing & Shibuya Sky

Cross the scramble a few times for content, then go up for sunset views. Book Sky tickets ahead.

19:00

Shinjuku at Night

Golden Gai's tiny bars, Omoide Yokocho's yakitori alley, and the neon labyrinth of Kabukicho. Don't follow the touts.

21:30

Late-Night Izakaya

End the day with karaage and highballs. You've earned it. Probably a few times over.

Day 3 — Culture & Big Views

Chill Before You Collapse

08:30

Tsukiji Outer Market

Fresh sushi breakfast and market chaos. Go hungry, leave in a food coma.

10:30

Imperial Palace Gardens

Free, calm, and full of joggers who make you feel unfit. Walk the East Gardens.

13:00

teamLab Planets

Get lost in digital art for two hours. Bring socks you're willing to get wet, seriously.

16:00

Shopping: Ginza or Odaiba

Ginza for high-end department stores, Odaiba for mall + waterfront + Gundam statue. Pick your poison.

19:00

Tokyo Tower at Night

Retro, iconic, and arguably prettier than the Skytree. Sunset to blue hour is the sweet spot.

21:00

Final Dinner: Omakase or Tonkatsu

Spend your last yen on either the best sushi of your life or a flawless pork cutlet. No wrong answers.

Survival Kit

expand for wisdom
Essential Travel Tips
  • IC Card: Get a Suica or Pasmo at any station. Tap for trains, buses, vending machines, and konbini purchases. Your life becomes 500% smoother.
  • Cash is King: Japan is modern, but cash still rules. Carry some yen; many small shops and all temple stalls are cash-only.
  • No Tipping: It's not expected and can actually confuse staff. Just say "arigato" and pay the exact amount. Wild, right?
  • Konbini = Lifesaver: 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson are open 24/7 and sell shockingly good food. Midnight egg sandwich? Yes.
  • Train Apps: Google Maps works fine. For extra credit, use Japan Transit Planner for hyper-precise routes.
  • Bowing & Quiet: Trains are library-quiet. Phone on silent, no calls. When in doubt, bow slightly and say "sumimasen" (excuse me / sorry).
Etiquette 101 (Don't Be That Tourist)
  • Slurping: Loudly slurping noodles is a compliment to the chef. Embrace it.
  • Chopsticks: Never stick them vertically into rice — it's a funeral ritual. Also no passing food chopstick-to-chopstick.
  • Shoes: Take them off in homes, ryokan, temples, and some restaurants. Look for the little shoe cupboard.
  • Bathrooms: The toilet paper is free but the "washlet symphony" of buttons can be terrifying. The one that looks like a tiny shower head? That's the bidet. Good luck.
  • Subway Etiquette: Let people off before boarding, and don't talk on the phone. Ever. The death stare is real.
Phrases That Save You
  • Sumimasen — excuse me / sorry (the Swiss army knife of Japanese)
  • Arigato gozaimasu — thank you very much
  • Kore o kudasai — "this one, please" (point and survive)
  • Oishii — delicious (say this after every meal, they love it)
  • Toire wa doko desu ka? — where is the bathroom? (you will need this)
